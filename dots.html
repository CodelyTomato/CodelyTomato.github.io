Design of an Otto Cycle Engine for a Hybrid Electric Car

MEMS 3430 Final Project, 2026

Myself and three other students contributed to make the final project for Design of Thermal Systems, where we analyzed and designed an Otto Cycle for use in a Hybrid Electric Car. It was a very interesting problem to solve, because we found out quickly that a pretty sophisticated controls scheme is used due to the variability of efficiency in hybrid vehicles.

The first big break came when me and Luke cracked the torque-power relationship for a combined power plant, which created a usable design space. It took some estimation based on available relationships (and some filling in the gaps of predicted results). My main contribution to the report is the driving schedules, which utilized a python script that I wrote breaking up the times the gasoline engine is used, the electric motor is used, and when both can be used together. Also shown is the periods of time when the battery can be regenerated by the gasoline engine, and the battery state of charge. The plots ended up being very pretty.
