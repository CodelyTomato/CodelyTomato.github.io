  3. MEMS 3430 - Design of Thermal Systems

MEMS 3430 focused on the analysis and design of advanced thermo-fluid and energy systems, including power generating cycles, refrigeration and air conditioning cycles, thermodynamic cycles used for transportation, phase change and heat exchangers, and renewable energy sources utilizing thermal energy. As part of the course, I worked on three individual design projects, as well as a collaborative final project. One of my favorite courses because of the emphasize on real-world applications as well as real engineering principlies, mainly the use of making assumptions and justifying them.

Final Project, Design of an Otto Cycle Engine for a Hybrid Electric Vehicle

My main contribution was to the final project was Section 7.3, the creation of a simulation of different drive cycles using a control scheme that selected between when to use only the electric motor, only the gasoline engine, or a mix of both. It also included fuel status and battery state-of-charge in order to accurately model the situations faced by real hybrid vehicles. I also used both highway and urban driving cycles from the EPA due to the different driving situations in both circumstances. Using the term "control-scheme" is a little exaggerated, since all I really did was create a lot of if statements.
